Step-by-Step Mathematical Sampling Pipeline

Live Mathematical Proof
How raw neural network logits transform into sampling probabilities:
Step 1

Raw Logits (z_i)

The neural network's final linear projection layer outputs unnormalized scores called logits for every token in vocabulary V:

z = [z_1, z_2, ..., z_N] ∈ (-∞, +∞)

Logits can be any real number: negative, zero, or positive. They cannot be interpreted as probabilities because they don't sum to 1 and can be negative.

Step 2

Temperature Scaling (z_i / T)

Temperature T > 0 scales each logit prior to exponentiation, controlling the steepness of the probability landscape:

z'_i = \frac{z_i}{T}
When T → 0 (Cold):

Differences between logits are magnified to infinity. The maximum logit gets probability 1.0; all others become 0.0 (Deterministic / Argmax).

When T = 1.0 (Standard):

Original model distribution. Represents the exact calibrated log-likelihood of the trained model.

When T → ∞ (Hot):

Differences between logits are crushed to zero (z_i / T → 0, e^0 = 1). All tokens become equally probable (Uniform distribution).

Step 3

Softmax & Numerical Stability (Log-Sum-Exp Trick)

The standard Softmax formula normalizes exponentials:

σ(z)_i = \frac{e^{z_i / T}}{\sum_{j=1}^N e^{z_j / T}}
⚠️ The Numerical Overflow Trap & Solution:

In IEEE 754 floating-point arithmetic, e^710 overflows to +Infinity, leading to Infinity / Infinity = NaN crashes. To prevent this, production LLMs apply the Log-Sum-Exp shift trick: subtract the maximum logit m = max(z_j / T):

σ(z)_i = \frac{e^{(z_i / T) - m}}{\sum_{j=1}^N e^{(z_j / T) - m}}

Mathematical Proof of Equivalence:
\frac{e^{z_i - m}}{\sum e^{z_j - m}} = \frac{e^{z_i} \cdot e^{-m}}{\sum e^{z_j} \cdot e^{-m}} = \frac{e^{z_i} \cdot \cancel{e^{-m}}}{\sum e^{z_j} \cdot \cancel{e^{-m}}} = \frac{e^{z_i}}{\sum e^{z_j}}.
Because the largest exponent is now e^0 = 1.0, overflow is mathematically impossible!

Step 4

Top-K Filtering

Sort tokens by probability descending. Keep only the top K candidates, zeroing all ranks > K, then renormalize:

p'_i = \begin{cases} \frac{p_i}{\sum_{k \in \text{Top-K}} p_k} & \text{if } \text{rank}(i) \le K \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}

Limitation: A fixed K is rigid. If the model is confident with only 1 good token, K=50 still includes 49 bad tokens. If the model is uncertain between 100 good tokens, K=10 truncates 90 valid candidates.

Step 5

Top-P (Nucleus) Sampling

Top-P dynamically adapts vocabulary size. It finds the smallest subset of tokens whose cumulative probability exceeds threshold P ∈ (0, 1]:

V^{(P)} = \text{smallest set such that } \sum_{i \in V^{(P)}} p_i \ge P

Why Nucleus is superior:
• In high-confidence states (e.g., "The capital of France is ___"), the nucleus contracts to just 1 or 2 tokens.
• In low-confidence states (e.g., "The strange creature had a ___"), the nucleus dynamically expands to dozens of plausible tokens!
